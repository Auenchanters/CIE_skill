# Observed exam patterns (MSRIT, first semester, 2025–26 and 2026–27)

Starting assumptions only, from one AI&DS (physics-cycle) student's papers. Re-check with the student's own papers; other sections, cycles and teachers can differ.

## General
- CIE-1: 30 marks, 1 hour. Most papers: 3 questions × 15, answer any two (C makes Q1 compulsory). Schedules list portions as lesson ranges (e.g. "L1–L13"); ask for the official CIE syllabus sheet instead of guessing.
- Papers print a Bloom level (L1 remember, L2 understand, L3 apply) and CO per part. L3 parts are programs, numericals, expression evaluation and "apply to this context" answers.
- "Differentiate between…" and compound parts (two topics in one 5-marker) are common.
- SEE: 100 marks, 3 hours, one full question from each of 5 units (2 choices each). Humanities SEE: 50 marks.
- Departments publish question banks, model papers (CIE-I/II/III, SEE models) and tutorials. Model papers state "questions from model papers may not appear".

## Maths (Calculus & Linear Algebra, MAC11)
- CIE format: 3 questions × 15 (2 + 3 + 5 + 5), answer any two; each question mixes units.
- QB verbatim rate: 5/12 in both 2025 and 2026 CIE-1. Model papers: weak predictor.
- CIE-1 2026 asked: polar → Cartesian; Taylor series about x = a; orthogonal surfaces (QB II-19); functional dependence via Jacobian; u_tt − c²u_xx; Euler log form (QB I-26); ∇·(r/r³) (QB II-45); e^{2x}log(1+y) expansion; du/dx implicit (QB I-31d); r = √(x²+y²) second-derivative proof; irrotational a, b + potential (QB II-38); maxima/minima.

## Physics (Quantum Physics & Applications, PYC12)
- CIE format: 3 questions × 15 (7 + 5 + 3), answer any two. The 3-mark part is always a numerical.
- Repeats last year's CIE-1 theory almost verbatim (He–Ne laser, Einstein coefficients/energy density, NA derivation, attenuation causes, electron not in nucleus).
- Numericals reuse tutorial templates with new numbers (modes/V-number, attenuation, uncertainty Δx).
- CIE-1 portion (2026): Unit I photonics (lasers, holography, LIDAR, Mach–Zehnder, fibres) + Unit II QM up to "non-existence of electron in nucleus (relativistic)". No Schrödinger/well/tunnelling in CIE-1.

## C programming (PSCCS14)
- CIE format: Q1 compulsory (a, b, c × 5), then Q2 or Q3 (3 × 5). Portion 2026: L1–L18 (Unit I computing/overview of C/expressions + Unit II console I/O and statements). No arrays.
- Q1 always has a trace-type part: "write the output" (2025) or "evaluate step by step considering precedence and associativity" (2026).
- CIE-1 2026 asked: Q1 (a) identifier: definition, rules, classify `num2`, `$num1`, `+add`, `a_2`; (b) evaluate `x + y * z / x - y % z` (x=3, y=6, z=10 → 17) and `(a>b) && (b<c) || (a+c>b)` (a=8, b=12, c=6 → 1); (c) switch-case calculator. Q2 (a) differentiate compiler/interpreter and high-level/machine language; (b) console I/O, getchar/putchar with a program; (c) find the errors in 5 snippets. Q3 (a) basic data types + storage classes; (b) electricity bill (first 200 units ₹5, next 200 ₹7, above 400 ₹10); (c) do-while vs while.
- Repeats across papers: identifier rules, switch calculator, storage classes, while vs do-while, formatted/unformatted I/O, "find the error". Lab programs return with new numbers. "Structure of a C program" was in 3/3 earlier papers but not in 2026.

## Mechanical (Intro to Mechanical Engineering, ESC134)
- CIE format: 3 questions × 15 (7/8 + 7/8), answer any two. Portion 2026: Unit 1 & 2 (L1–L12).
- **The teacher's Assignment 1 predicted the whole 2026 paper (6/6).** Last year's CIE-1 predicted 0/6 verbatim.
- CIE-1 2026 asked: role of ME in manufacturing industries and marine sectors (8); ferrous vs non-ferrous with examples (7); hydroelectric plant with schematic (7); thermal plant sketch + working + 2 advantages/disadvantages (8); define lathe + working principle (7); sketch and explain any two lathe operations (8).
- CIE-1 2025 asked: nuclear plant; role of ME in energy and marine; material classification + applications; taper turning by swivelling the compound rest; drilling/milling operations (knurling, reaming and counter-boring, up vs down milling).
- Textbook: K. R. Gopalakrishna et al., *Elements of Mechanical Engineering* (Sudha). Use its material classification (Metals → ferrous/non-ferrous; Non-metallic → synthetic/natural) and figures. The teacher's Unit-2 document (lathe, drilling, milling) has the class figures; embed them.
- Syllabus note: "no sketches of machine tools; sketches only to explain operations", but the assignment and paper still ask for lathe sketches, so include both.

## Electronics (IEC, ESC133): not yet scored
- From last year's CIE-1: every question had one Unit-I and one Unit-IV part. Bridge rectifier, voltage doubler and full adder from 2 half adders came almost verbatim; Zener design, number conversions/complements and Boolean simplification came as templates with new numbers.

## Kannada Kali (HSCP16K, for non-Kannada speakers)
- Written entirely in Kannada using English letters (transliteration). 30 marks, 1 hour, Units 1 & 2.
- Part A (6 × 1, L1): fill-in MCQs on question words (eenu/yaaru/yaavudu), relation words (snehite, tande, magaLu), locative case (BengaLurinalli), question form (chennagideyaa?), respectful pronouns (ivaru for a father).
- Part B: answer any two of three questions, each 6 + 6:
  - Q1: write the vowels and consonants (swaragaLu, vyanjanagaLu); write 12 lines about yourself.
  - Q2: translate 6 questions to English and answer them in Kannada (parents' names, sub-dialects of Kannada, the five Dravidian languages, friends' names, own or rented house, your name and native place); English meanings + a sentence for 12 words.
  - Q3: rearrange 6 jumbled sentences; brief introduction to the Kannada language.
- Prepare: a memorised 12-line self-introduction, a 6–8 line introduction to Kannada, the alphabet chart, a vocabulary list (family, places, question words, pronouns) each with meaning and one sentence, and jumbled-sentence practice using subject–object–verb order.

## Soft Skills (HSCP15): not yet scored
- From last year's CIE-1: 6 MCQs; a case study (4 + 4) on persuasion and leading change; 8-mark L3 questions per unit (three pillars of persuasion, SWOC analysis for a teacher or leader, stress techniques and their physiological vs psychological effects including time-outs, Eisenhower matrix for teachers/administrators).
- Questions ask to apply a concept to a given context. Answers: one-line definition, the named concept terms applied to that context in numbered points, conclusion.
- Last year's paper asked past the notes' "CIE syllabus ends here" line (Eisenhower matrix, adaptability).
